title: Relative Clauses and Reading Coherence

First, let us examine how a relative clause adds information to a noun. A defining relative clause identifies which person or thing the writer means, while a non-defining clause adds supplementary information and is normally separated by commas. The relative pronoun has a grammatical function inside the clause. Who and whom refer to people, which refers to things, and that can often refer to either in defining clauses. Students should not choose a pronoun merely because the noun before it looks familiar; they must check whether the missing element functions as a subject, an object, or a possessive modifier.

Next, we need to distinguish sentence-level grammar from discourse-level meaning. A grammatically correct sentence may still be inappropriate in a paragraph if its topic does not connect with the surrounding ideas. Pronouns, repeated key terms, parallel structures, and transition signals create cohesion. However, a transition word does not manufacture logic by itself. Therefore usually introduces a result, whereas however marks contrast, and for example moves from a general statement to a particular case.

When reading a longer passage, begin by identifying the subject of each paragraph and the change from one paragraph to the next. Writers may define a problem, describe its causes, compare possible explanations, and finally evaluate a solution. Details are not equally important. Dates, names, and numerical evidence may support a claim, but the main idea explains why those details appear. If an unfamiliar word occurs, examine its part of speech, nearby examples, contrast markers, and word parts before reaching for a dictionary.

Finally, inference must remain limited by evidence. A reasonable inference combines what the passage states with ordinary background knowledge, but it must not introduce a new story. In examination questions, extreme options containing words such as always, completely, or impossible often claim more than the text supports. The careful reader keeps returning to the wording, compares the scope of each option, and selects the interpretation that accounts for the greatest amount of evidence with the fewest unsupported assumptions.
